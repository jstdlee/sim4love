# sim4love — Core Values

This is the moral spine of the game. Every chapter, choice, lesson card, bad end and ending must reinforce at least
one value below, and must never contradict any of them. If a scene is fun but breaks a value, rewrite the scene.

> **One-line core:** Love other people, love yourself more. Your own life is the main story; a partner joins it,
> never replaces it.

## 1. The ten values

| # | Value | What the player should walk away believing | How the game shows it (not tells it) |
|---|---|---|---|
| V1 | **Self-respect first** | Liking someone never requires shrinking yourself. Self-respect is not selfishness. | Self-respect stat; options that beg, grovel or abandon your own limits always cost more than they gain |
| V2 | **Your life is the main story** | Your goals, passion, health, money and friends are yours to keep, in a relationship or not. A partner is a big part of life, never all of it. | The **Your Life** track (goal, passion, health, wallet) is visible all game and weighs most in the endings |
| V3 | **Invest where it grows** | Energy poured into chasing or pleasing one person returns little. Energy put into your craft, study, career and body compounds. | Free-evening choices: time on your passion/goal keeps paying off; extra time spent chasing someone has diminishing returns |
| V4 | **Never spend beyond your means for anyone** | Buying affection with money you don't have is absurd, not romantic. Attention that has to be paid for is a product. | Real wallet balance; no option where overspending is the best outcome; debt and "top gifter" bad ends |
| V5 | **Looks are packaging** | A polished exterior says nothing about character. Behind every beautiful feed is an ordinary person, sometimes with a messy room, bad habits, or a private life that is falling apart. Judge conduct over time, not appearance. | "Behind the filter" scenes (camp mornings, Yuna's burnt-out persona, Kai's rented car, polished classmates who borrow and never repay) |
| V6 | **Clear-headed, never obsessed** | Most of an infatuation is your own projection, plus sacrifices nobody asked you for. Check facts before feelings run the show. | The **Rose Filter** mechanic: idealising raises it and hides better options; fact-checking lowers it |
| V7 | **Not materialistic** | Status, brands and "high-value" lifestyles are marketing. They don't make you lovable, and they don't make anyone else worth more. | Alpha Kai's rented lifestyle, installment watches, luxury-on-credit dates. The cheap, thoughtful option always wins |
| V8 | **Respect without worship, disenchantment without contempt** | Nobody is a goddess, a prize or a villain. People are equals: flawed, ordinary and deserving of basic respect. Seeing through the shine is not a reason to look down on anyone. | Heroines are full people with flaws and goals; mocking a heroine is always a mistake; pedestal options are always a mistake too |
| V9 | **Bodies need a clear yes, and protection** | Consent is asked for, ongoing and can be withdrawn. Waiting is normal. Your "no" counts as much as theirs. Impulse is not a plan. Protection and testing are part of respect. | Ask options never cost anything; "not yet" is always respected; sex-ed facts in Part V; refusal practised in several contexts |
| V10 | **Healthy is the best outcome** | Honest, respectful, self-respecting choices produce the best story, including the most romantic one. | Scoring: the ✅ option is always best overall; the top ending is reachable on every route, including solo |

## 2. Disenchantment, done right

The game deliberately dismantles the "she is everything" fantasy. It does this through **de-idealisation**, never
through contempt.

**Do**
- Show ordinary reality behind glamour: the idol who lives on energy drinks and can't cook, the cool senpai who
  is behind on every assignment, the perfect feed that hides loneliness and debt.
- Make the player's projection visible: the game shows what he *assumed* next to what he actually *knows*.
- Let the "reveal" end in empathy or in a boundary, never in a sneer ("she's a person who's bad at sleeping",
  not "she's a fraud").
- Apply the same lens to men: Alpha Kai's rented Lamborghini, Takumi the polished student-council president who
  takes credit for others' work, Ren the stylish classmate who borrows rent and never repays.
- Keep the player as the main source of mistakes: obsession, overspending and self-neglect are *his* choices.

**Don't (hard rules)**
- Never describe women, or anyone, as sexual objects, or reduce a person to their body.
- Never compare attractive women (or anyone) to sex workers, and never use sex work as an insult.
- Never frame women in general as scammers, gold-diggers or manipulators. Trap behaviours are labelled as
  behaviours, and male characters use them too.
- Never reward mocking a heroine, body-shaming, or "punishing" a heroine for not matching the player's fantasy.
- Never glamorise the harem setup. Being surrounded by attractive people is the comedy premise, not the reward.

## 3. Ten things the player should be able to say after the game

1. "I can like her a lot and still keep my own plans."
2. "If I have to pay to be noticed, I'm a customer, not a partner."
3. "I don't actually know her yet. I know her photos."
4. "Pretty isn't a personality. How someone treats people is."
5. "My hours go to my goals first."
6. "No means no, not yet means not yet, and my no counts too."
7. "Condoms protect against pregnancy *and* most STIs. Many STIs have no symptoms, so we get tested."
8. "Anyone rushing me for money, photos or secrecy is running a script."
9. "I'm not spending money I don't have to impress anyone."
10. "A breakup hurts. It doesn't make me less."

## 4. Writing checklist (run on every chapter)

- [ ] Which value(s) does each interaction reinforce? (CHAPTERS.md lists them per chapter.)
- [ ] Is the ✅ option the best total outcome, and the most romantic one where romance is involved?
- [ ] Does any line glamorise worship, possession, or spending to win someone? Rewrite it.
- [ ] Does any line show contempt for women, objectify anyone, or generalise about a gender? Remove it.
- [ ] Does every de-idealisation reveal end in empathy or a boundary, not a sneer?
- [ ] Is the lesson *shown* through consequence before the lesson card names it? Lesson cards: two lines max.
- [ ] Serious topic (scams, sextortion, sexual health, alcohol, mental health)? End with a `{resource:…}` card.
